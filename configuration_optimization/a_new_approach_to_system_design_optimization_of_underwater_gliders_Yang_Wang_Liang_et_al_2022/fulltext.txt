A New Approach to System Design Optimization
of Underwater Gliders
Ming Yang , Yanhui Wang , Member, IEEE, Yan Liang , and Cheng Wang

Abstract—System design optimization of an underwater glider involves various disciplines, such as hydrodynamics, structure, sizing, control, power, payload, and trajectory, which overlap with each other and influence the
endurance of the system. Compared with traditional optimization methods, multidisciplinary design optimization
provides a more efficient method for complex engineering
problems involving overlapping disciplines. This article establishes a universal disciplinary framework of an underwater glider after the system analysis, which involves five
disciplines, including hydrodynamic shape, pressure hull,
buoyancy, attitude, and energy. To decouple the relationship among these disciplines and improve the efficiency
of optimization, approximate models in the disciplines of
hydrodynamic shape and pressure hull are established. After the coupling analysis, a novel approach is proposed for
the system design optimization of an underwater glider, in
which the concurrent subspace optimization, penalty function method, and the multipopulation genetic algorithm are
combined together. The approach proposed in this article
can meet the diversified design requirements of underwater gliders in different observation missions, which is
verified by a sea trial of an improved Petrel-L glider.
Index Terms—Multidisciplinary design optimization, system design optimization, underwater glider (UG).

a parametric geometric model of blend-wing-body UG and
optimized the hydrodynamic shape using the efficient global
optimization. Deutsch et al. [11] investigated the performance of
shape optimization with a different number of sectional airfoils

Although the above studies provide some optimization methods or results about UG design, they mainly focus on the effect of
a single discipline on the performance of UG but fail to consider
the complex coupling relationship among different disciplines.
Thus, the optimization results in the above studies may no longer
be the global optimum when the change in other disciplines
is involved.
Multidisciplinary design optimization (MDO) provides an
effective way in complex engineering applications, which can
coordinate all disciplines and decouple their interactions in the
design cycle so that designers can simultaneously improve the
design and reduce the time cost [18]. Currently, many MDO architectures have been proposed, concerning monolithic architectures and distributed architectures. Monolithic architectures use
a single optimizer for the whole optimization problem, including
multidisciplinary feasible (MDF) [19], individual discipline feasible [20], all-at-one [21], and simultaneous analysis and design
[22]. Distributed architectures divide the whole optimization
problem into many smaller discipline optimization problems and
manage interdisciplinary consistency by system-level coordination, mainly including CSSO [23], collaborative optimization
(CO) [24], bilevel-integrated system synthesis [25], and analytical target cascading [26].
Over the past decades, MDO theory has been increasingly
applied in the integrated design of AUV involving multiple
disciplines. Wang [27] adopted an MDO framework integrating
the CO method and approximate model technology to optimize the key parameters of UG. Luo and Lyu [28] adopted
the CO method and approximate model technology to optimize the hydrodynamic shape of the underwater robot, which
is realized by particle swarm optimization (PSO), an artificial intelligence technique. Zhang [29] established a multidisciplinary performance analysis model of a small intelligent
ocean exploration underwater vehicle, which involves six disciplines including the hull form, structure, propulsion, energy,
maneuverability, and general arrangement. Bidoki [30] proposed
a new optimization design framework, in which MDF as an

3495

MDO framework and PSO as an optimizer were combined
together for optimal and feasible conceptual design of an AUV.
Chen [31] adopted MDF architecture and a gradient-based optimization algorithm to design an AUV with the purpose of
minimizing the energy consumption with a predefined sailing
distance.
However, few works of literature have introduced the application of the MDO theory in system design optimization of
UG, in which two major challenges exist: 1) The whole UG
system involves the disciplines of hydrodynamic shape, pressure
hull, buoyancy, attitude, energy, and so on, which overlap with
each other to jointly influence the system performance. It is
difficult to achieve the optimum design if these disciplines are
not synthetically considered. 2) System design optimization
aims to achieve the wider application of UGs in ocean science
research. Due to the variety of the observation missions and the
sensors onboard, a series of UGs, involving different dimensions
and operation depths, need to be developed, and the universal
optimization model needs to be studied.
To fill this research gap, this article proposes a novel approach
for system design optimization of UG, which is performed
in three steps. First, the layout of UG is determined and the
gliding range model is established. Then, the discipline analysis
is carried out and the disciplines involved are modeled. Finally, a
coupling analysis based on the above models is executed and an
optimization framework is constructed to achieve the optimum
design. The contributions of this article are summarized as
follows:
1) A universal discipline classification and the modeling
involved in the system design of UG are described in
detail, which provides a reference for UG designers.
2) A mission-requirement-based optimization architecture
of UG is established for the first time. By importing
the load capacity, operation depth, hotel load, and other
indexes of UG required in a specific observation mission,
an optimum design scheme can be obtained.
3) The MDO theory is used in design optimization of the
whole UG system for the first time to decouple interactions among disciplines and achieve the optimum design.
4) The effectiveness of the approach proposed in this article
is demonstrated by massive data obtained in a long-term
operation of an improved Petrel-L.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. Section II
introduces the modeling of UG, including the layout and gliding
range model of UG. In Section III, the discipline classification
and modeling are described after the system analysis. Section IV
builds an optimization model based on the discipline classification and modeling. Then, the optimization is performed and the
results are discussed in Section V. In Section VI, a sea trial is
carried out to verify the approach proposed in this article. Finally,
Section VII concludes the article.

effect on the total gliding range of UG. The work pressure Pw
is set as 10 MPa, and the hull material is 6061T6 alloy. The

In the MPGA method, the control parameters of each population including the crossover operator and mutation operator are
set to be different in order to ensure the global search capability
and local search capability, the values of which are selected
randomly from the ranges of 0.7 to 0.9 and 0.001 to 0.05, respectively. The individual number is 80 and the population number
is 10. Considering the consistency of optimization results under
the same condition, the maximum number of iteration is set as
100 after trying to solve for many times.

Fig. 11 shows the optimization results, from which the following analysis results can be obtained.
1) The total gliding range of UG nonlinearly decreases and
gradually stabilizes with the hotel load Psc .
2) The total gliding range increases with the proportionality
coefficient pr , and the trend is more obvious when the
hotel load Psc is smaller.
3) The total gliding range decreases with the net mass Bsm ,
and the trend is more obvious when the proportionality
coefficient pr is smaller.
Fig. 12 gives the trends of the optimal design variables when
Bsm is 1 kg, in which the trend of wing position Cl is not
expressed due to its irregular trend. The following conclusions
can be drawn.
1) The values of the optimal pitch angles, including θ1
and θ2 , increase with hotel load Psc , and decrease with
proportionality coefficient pr .
2) The optimal oil volumes, including δVin and δVout1 ,
increase with hotel load Psc and proportionality
coefficient pr .
3) The optimal wing dimensions, including Ct , Cl, and Cs ,
decrease with hotel load Psc , and increase with proportionality coefficient pr .
4) The optimal hull lengths, including l1 and l2 , do not
change with hotel load Psc , and increase with proportionality coefficient pr .

Similarly, the optimization results of UG with other work
pressures Pw can also be obtained by the proposed approach.
In the field of practical engineering, based on the requirements
of an observation mission, the system design scheme of UG can
be rapidly obtained by the proposed approach. First, the work
pressure Pw , total gliding range RUG , hotel load Psc and net mass
Bsm of UG can be obtained from the trial position, trial path,
and sensors carried by UG in a specific observation mission.
Then corresponding Fig. 11 can be obtained by the approach
proposed in this article, and the proportionality coefficient pr
can be determined according to total gliding range RUG . Finally,
by importing the above indexes into the optimization model, the
design variables in Fig. 12 can be optimized for designing the
UG system.
Compared with approaches proposed in our previous work
for motion parameter optimization [2] and hydrodynamic shape
optimization [9] of UG, the optimization method in this article
considers the coupling relationship among the different disciplines and realizes a collaborative optimization of these design
parameters, which will further increase the gliding range of UG.
Specifically, under the conditions of the same system layout,
hull structure, and the total mass, the increased rate of gliding
range caused by the optimization approach in this article is
contrasted with the optimization results in our previous work,
shown in Fig. 13. The comparison results indicate that the system
optimization approach proposed in this article has a significant
advantage for increasing the gliding range of UG.

VII. CONCLUSION
In this article, the layout and the gliding range model of UG
are introduced, and the universal model of UG considering its
displacement and operation depth is established, which involves
the five disciplines, hydrodynamic shape, pressure hull, buoyancy, attitude, and energy. Based on the CSSO-PFM-MPGA
architecture involving the approximate models, the design parameters of UG can be rapidly determined for maximizing the
total gliding range after giving the requirement of UG in a
specific observation mission. The optimization results and the
optimal design parameters are analyzed when the work pressure
of UG is set as 10 MPa, and the validity of the optimization
model is verified by a sea trial of an improved Petrel-L. The
