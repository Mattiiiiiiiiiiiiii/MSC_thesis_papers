Abstract— This work proposes a modified analytical model
for flux linkage and torque calculation in Switched Reluctance
Machines (SRM). This model employs special features of the
SRM and BH curve models to determine flux linkage with knee
modification. The analytical model also considers the basic
geometric parameters like turns, phase current, and stator and
rotor pole tapering angles that affect average torque and
current density. The analytical model of main and fringing flux
linkages is integrated to calculate co-energy and static torque as
a function of rotor position. The BH curve model with knee
adjustment and tapering angles was added to help estimate
torque profiles more accurately. The flux linkage and torque
profiles derived from the analytical model are verified using
FEA Finite element analysis (FEA) and experimental
measurements of an 18 slot/12 pole, 32 kW SRM.
Keywords—Switched reluctance motor, analytical model, flux
linkage, torque.

I.

INTRODUCTION

Switched Reluctance Machine (SRM) is becoming
popular for Electric Vehicles (EVs) and Hybrid Electric
Vehicles (HEVs) applications due to its low cost, fault
tolerance capability, and equivalent efficiency as other
topologies [1]. The performance of SRM is dictated by the
material properties, design methodology, and control
techniques. Because of its nonlinear behavior, control of
SRM is difficult. The nonlinear relationship between rotor
position, flux linkage, and phase currents determine torque
output in SRMs [2]. To account for this non-linear behavior
a detailed analysis and design optimization of SRM's
performance should be carried out. Finite element analysis
(FEA) and analytical methods can be used to perform
electromagnetic analysis of SRMs. The time required for
design optimization using FEA is high, whereas analytical
models consume less time for computation. However,
analytical modeling of SRM helps in the faster and real-time
prediction of machine performance only if its’ accuracy is
comparable to FEA.
Based on FEA or experimental data, authors have
computed flux linkage and torque using lookup tables,
interpolation, and other techniques in [3-5]. Running many
simulations and tests is also required to obtain the data from
FEA or experiment. Researchers have created analytical
models in [6, 7] that are based on the SRM geometry and the
Fourier series to calculate flux linkage and torque. The error
in estimating the flux linkage has decreased with these
methods, however, the inaccuracy in calculating the torque at
partially overlapped positions is still significant. To
determine unaligned inductance and flux linkage as a function
of rotor position and fundamental geometric factors, the
mathematical model is developed in [8, 9] by A. Radun (AR).

rotor positions. The flux linkage is computed in this model
using the special features of the SRM and BH curve models
with knee adjustment. The inputs to this model are the
